How to cite

The references button wrote this text and the file example.bib beside this document; the header above names the citation style and that file. Delete the text once you have seen what it shows.

A key in brackets cites a work, and the style decides how it prints (Doe and Roe 2020). A page number follows the key after a comma (Smith 2019, 3), and the other locators work the same way, such as a chapter (Smith 2019, chap. 2) or a figure (Doe and Roe 2020, fig. 1). Words before the key are a prefix and words after the locator a suffix, both printed with the citation (see Kim 2018, chap. 2, for the method). Several works cite together when semicolons separate them (Doe and Roe 2020; Smith 2019).

A key written without brackets makes the author part of the sentence, as Lee (2021) shows, and a bracket right after it holds the locator, as Smith (2019, 7) shows. A minus sign before the key leaves the author out when the sentence names them already: Kim (2018) compared the styles. A footnote can cite as well.[1]

In the live view a citation shows as the style prints it; put the cursor on its line to see and edit the source. Type @ to pick a key from the list of references, and rest the pointer on a key to see its entry. The reference list stands where the refs block below is; without a block it goes to the end of the document, under the heading the header's reference-section-title gives it.
